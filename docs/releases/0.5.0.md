# XenoNet 0.5.0

The map now shows the other machines in your network as they are, and you can open their sessions' transcripts from XenoNet's window. A machine can publish a local service to the network, so programs on the other machines reach it without opening a port. On a Mac, a menu bar app shows what needs you while Desktop is closed. XenoNet calls Xenolith "Shared context" and reads its folder by Xenolith's published format.

Desktop, Tools and the Claude Code plugin are 0.5.0, the Service is 0.5.1 and the Mac tray is 0.4.1. [Updating](#updating) says what to do.

## Other machines on the map

- While XenoNet's window is open on a window host, each other machine in the network is drawn from its own map: its projects, sessions and context pages, and bridges between clones of one repo on two machines. A machine that goes offline dims and keeps what it showed last.
- Select one of another machine's sessions to see its state and what waits on you there, open its transcript and stats, and message it. Transcripts and stats open only in XenoNet's window, never in a browser tab or for an agent, and nothing keeps them.
- A machine sends its map only to a window host, and only while that console keeps asking, every 20 seconds. With **Share with the network: titles only** on, its map goes with titles, states and counts only.

[You, at any machine](https://github.com/willctl/xenonet/blob/main/docs/guide/network.md#you-at-any-machine) has the details.

## Services through the network

A machine can publish a local HTTP service to its network, and programs on the other machines call it through their own XenoNet. XenoNet carries each request and its answer, sealed like every frame, and knows nothing of what the service is.

```bash
xenonet svc publish files http://127.0.0.1:8080 --paths /api/v1/      # on studio
curl http://127.0.0.1:47840/api/svc/studio/files/api/v1/items          # on another machine
```

- Publishing and taking a service off are yours to do: XenoNet refuses them from under an agent. A service is reachable only at the path prefixes you name with `--paths`, and `--hosts` narrows which machines may call it.
- The service gets the method, path, query and body, and only `Authorization`, `Content-Type`, `Accept` and `User-Agent` of the caller's headers. `Authorization` passes untouched, so the service's own tokens work. XenoNet adds `Forwarded: by=xenonet;for=<machine>`, naming the calling machine as its signature proved it.
- A request is never stored on the way, on the gateway or anywhere else, since it may carry the service's credential. When either machine isn't linked, the caller gets 503 at once. A request's body is at most 1 MiB and an answer's 4 MiB, and each machine may call each service 300 times a minute.
- **A published service with no sign-in of its own is open to every program on every machine allowed to call it.** Give it tokens of its own, or name the machines with `--hosts`.

This is how Xenolith syncs shared context between machines with no open port. [Services through the network](https://github.com/willctl/xenonet/blob/main/docs/guide/network.md#services-through-the-network) has the details and the Xenolith example.

## The tray on a Mac

XenoNet Tray is a menu bar app that shows whether XenoNet runs and what needs you while Desktop is closed: each item in the console's words, which opens the console at it, then Open console, the gateway's state and the network's link. It has no Dock icon.

- It comes with the disk image. Turning on the background service, in **Background service** or with `xenonet service install`, copies it to `~/Applications/XenoNet Tray.app` and starts it at login.
- While it runs, Desktop hides its own menu bar icon. Windows and Linux keep Desktop's tray.
- It works only from `/Applications` or `~/Applications`. The service gives it its key only when it can see that the program asking is that app, not something an agent started.

[The tray on macOS](https://github.com/willctl/xenonet/blob/main/docs/guide/always-on.md#the-tray-on-macos) has the details.

## Shared context

- The map, legend, panels, palette and doctor call Xenolith's layer "Shared context" and its pages "context pages", instead of "wiki".
- XenoNet reads Xenolith's folder by [FORMAT.md](https://github.com/willctl/xenonet/blob/main/xenolith/FORMAT.md), the format Xenolith publishes, and its `xenolith.json` marker. The marker names the MCP server, so calls to Xenolith under a name of your own show as beams too. A folder in a later format than this XenoNet reads stays on the map without its pages, and its panel says to update XenoNet.
- Xenolith ships on its own. [Xenolith 1.9.0](https://github.com/willctl/xenonet/releases/tag/xenolith-v1.9.0) installs from one download and moves an agent-wiki install over with `xenolith migrate agent-wiki`.

## Also

- Hermes Agent: XenoNet reads Hermes's session database through its schema 32. Its observer reads activity and edit metadata, not conversation text.
- The doctor's check for tokens left in repos warns only about tokens XenoNet issued that still work, not made-up ones in test fixtures.
- An update no longer fails its start-up check when the binary it just unpacked is briefly busy.
- [Managed Windows installation](https://github.com/willctl/xenonet/blob/main/docs/guide/managed-windows.md) covers installing on a computer that needs approval for each program, and Hermes in WSL.

## Updating

From 0.4.0:

1. Update Desktop. On Windows, open **Updates** and choose **Restart and update**. On a Mac, replace XenoNet in Applications with the copy from the new disk image, and open it. Your settings stay in place, and the running service and terminals carry on.
2. Update the Tools, then the Service: in **Updates**, or with `xenonet update tools` and then `xenonet update service`. If terminals are open in XenoNet, the service update waits until you close them and choose Update again.
3. On a Mac with the background service on, run `xenonet service install` from your own terminal once the Tools are 0.5.0. It puts the tray in `~/Applications` and starts it, and leaves the running service alone. With the background service off, turning it on sets up the tray.

Update every machine in a network. A machine still on 0.4.0 shows on the others' maps from its sessions alone, and can neither publish nor call a service.

Without Desktop, download the Service and Tools archives and run `./bin/xenonet setup` from the Tools folder, then from the Service folder.

`SHA256SUMS.txt` lists checksums for the downloads.
